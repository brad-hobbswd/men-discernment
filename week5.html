SESSION FIVE · STUDENT READING

Brotherhood That Can Handle the Truth

Honesty, Accountability, and Growth

Student Reading | Session Five

Proverbs 27:5–6, Galatians 6:1–2, Ephesians 4:15, Proverbs 17:17

Introduction

Strong brotherhood requires more than spending time together. Meaningful relationships create space for honesty, encouragement, correction, and accountability.

Many men are comfortable talking about everyday matters while finding personal conversations more difficult. Admitting struggles, receiving correction, or acknowledging mistakes can feel uncomfortable. Yet spiritual growth often requires conversations we might otherwise avoid.

Godly brotherhood is not built around pretending we have everything figured out. Brotherhood grows when men are willing to walk alongside one another, speak truthfully, and remain committed to one another’s spiritual growth.

Do I have relationships where I can be honest, and am I willing to become that kind of brother to someone else?

1. Honest Conversations Are Valuable

Proverbs 27:5–6

Honest correction can be an expression of genuine care. A brother who recognizes something harmful in our behavior and approaches us with sincerity may provide an opportunity for growth we would not have recognized on our own.

Correction is not always comfortable. Our first response may be defensiveness, embarrassment, or frustration. However, discomfort does not automatically mean the correction lacks value.

A mature man considers what is being said rather than immediately rejecting the person speaking.

Honesty requires both courage and compassion. Truth should not become an excuse for humiliating someone, and kindness should not become an excuse for avoiding necessary conversations.

2. Accountability Requires Humility

Galatians 6:1–2

Scripture emphasizes restoring someone with gentleness and helping one another carry burdens.

Accountability is not about controlling another man’s life, exposing private struggles, or establishing superiority. Accountability means caring enough to encourage spiritual growth, offer support, and speak honestly when necessary.

Receiving accountability also requires humility. We must be willing to acknowledge when we need help, when our behavior needs correction, and when our decisions are not producing the results we desire.

A healthy brotherhood recognizes that every man needs encouragement, prayer, wisdom, and support at different points in his life.

3. Speak Truth With Love

Ephesians 4:15

The manner in which truth is communicated matters.

A conversation intended to help can become harmful when delivered through anger, pride, sarcasm, or humiliation. The goal should be restoration and growth rather than winning an argument or proving ourselves right.

Before approaching someone, consider our motives. Are we seeking to help? Have we taken time to understand the situation? Are we willing to listen to the other person’s perspective?

Truth and love belong together. Our words should reflect a genuine desire for another man’s spiritual well being.

Personal Reflection

  • Am I comfortable receiving correction?
  • Do I have someone who can challenge me spiritually?
  • Have I avoided a necessary conversation?
  • When correcting someone, do my words reflect compassion and humility?
  • Am I willing to support another man without judging or attempting to control him?

Session Five Accountability

A Conversation That Builds Brotherhood

Today, intentionally encourage or connect with another man.

Ask how he is doing beyond the surface level. Listen carefully. Offer encouragement, prayer, or support when appropriate.

Consider whether there is a conversation you need to approach with greater honesty or humility.

Reflect afterward:

Did my conversation strengthen trust, encourage growth, or help someone feel supported?

Come prepared to share what you learned about honesty, encouragement, or accountability.
