SESSION SEVEN Wisdom When the Answer Is Not Obvious Making Decisions With Prayer, Patience, and Counsel Student Reading | Session Seven Key Scriptures: James 1:5, Proverbs 3:5–6, Proverbs 15:22, Isaiah 40:31 Introduction Some decisions are easy to make. Others require time, prayer, careful consideration, and wisdom. We may face circumstances where the right answer is not immediately clear, where several options appear reasonable, or where emotions make a decision difficult. Discernment does not mean we will always know exactly what to do. Discernment helps us seek God, evaluate our choices, and respond faithfully even when we do not have every answer. How do I respond when I am uncertain about what to do? 1. Seek God for Wisdom James 1:5 God invites us to seek wisdom. We do not have to pretend to know everything or make every decision independently. Prayer allows us to bring our concerns, questions, and uncertainty before God. Seeking wisdom also requires a willingness to listen, examine our motives, and allow Scripture to shape our decisions. Sometimes we want God to confirm the decision we have already made. Genuine wisdom requires remaining open to correction and direction. Seeking God should be part of our decision making rather than something we turn to only after our plans have failed. 2. Trust God Rather Than Relying Only on Our Understanding Proverbs 3:5–6 Our perspective is limited. We may understand part of a situation while remaining unaware of circumstances that could influence the outcome. Trusting God does not mean abandoning responsibility or refusing to think carefully. We should consider the facts, evaluate consequences, seek appropriate counsel, and pray. The difference is that we recognize our understanding is not the ultimate authority. When a decision feels uncertain, we can remain faithful without allowing anxiety to force an immediate answer. 3. Seek Wise Counsel and Practice Patience Proverbs 15:22; Isaiah 40:31 Wise counsel can help us recognize perspectives we may have overlooked. A trusted brother, spiritual leader, or mature believer may offer insight, encouragement, or correction. Seeking counsel does not mean asking everyone for an opinion until someone agrees with us. We should seek people who value truth, wisdom, spiritual maturity, and our long term growth. Some decisions also require patience. Waiting can be uncomfortable, especially when we want circumstances resolved immediately. However, rushing can lead us to decisions driven by frustration, fear, or pressure. Wisdom recognizes when action is necessary and when patience is the better response. Personal Reflection * Is there a decision I am currently struggling to make? * Have I sincerely prayed for wisdom concerning the matter? * Am I allowing fear, pressure, or frustration to influence my decision? * Have I sought counsel from someone who can offer honest and biblical guidance? * Am I willing to wait when wisdom requires patience? Session Seven Accountability A Decision Examined Through Wisdom Identify one decision you are facing or one decision you recently made. Take time to examine: What are the facts? What options are available? What does Scripture teach that applies to this decision? What motives or emotions may be influencing me? Whose wise counsel could help me? Pray specifically for wisdom and identify one responsible next step. Come prepared to share what you learned about making decisions through prayer, biblical principles, and wise counsel. Preparing for Session Eight Becoming Men Who Speak Life Discernment is not complete when we recognize truth or make wise decisions. The way we communicate, treat others, and carry our responsibilities reveals whether discernment is becoming part of our daily lives. Our final session will focus on applying discernment through our words, relationships, and conduct.
