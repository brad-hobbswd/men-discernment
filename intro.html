Introduction We spend much of our lives talking. We discuss work, family, responsibilities, relationships, church, finances, frustrations, plans, and the things happening around us. Conversations fill our days, yet some of the most important conversations are often the ones we never have. There are things men carry quietly. Questions we do not know how to ask. Decisions we struggle to make. Situations we replay in our minds. Relationships we wish were different. Moments when we recognize something is wrong, yet cannot quite identify what we are seeing. There are times when we know we need wisdom, but we are uncertain where to begin. Discernment becomes especially important during those moments. Discernment is more than recognizing something good or bad. Discernment involves understanding, examining, weighing, and responding according to the wisdom of God. Spiritual maturity requires more than reacting to what we feel, what we hear, or what appears obvious. We must learn to bring our thoughts, motives, circumstances, and conversations before the Lord. The Word of God teaches us that wisdom is something we pursue, understanding is something we seek, and spiritual maturity develops as we exercise our ability to distinguish between what is right and what is wrong. Those abilities do not develop through pride, assumption, or impulsive reactions. They develop through prayer, Scripture, obedience, humility, experience, and a willingness to learn. This teaching was developed around an important question: Are we taking the time to talk about the things that truly matter? Our conversations can reveal much about our spiritual condition. The subjects we avoid, the truths we resist, the assumptions we make, and the words we choose can reveal areas where God desires to work within us. Sometimes the most meaningful growth begins when we are willing to stop pretending, begin listening, and allow an honest conversation to take place. Throughout these eight weeks, we will examine discernment from a biblical perspective. We will consider the importance of understanding our own hearts, listening carefully, distinguishing truth from assumption, recognizing influences, receiving godly counsel, making wise decisions, and speaking in ways that strengthen others. This is not an invitation to become suspicious of everyone around us. Nor is this a study designed to encourage criticism, judgmental attitudes, or the constant examination of other people's failures. Biblical discernment begins with a willingness to let God examine us. The journey ahead is intended to bring men closer to the Lord, closer to biblical wisdom, and closer to one another through conversations that are honest, meaningful, and spiritually productive. We do not have to arrive at every gathering with every answer. We simply need to arrive willing to listen, willing to learn, willing to grow, and willing to allow the Word of God to speak. The greatest measure of discernment is not how quickly we identify the shortcomings of others. The greater measure is how faithfully we recognize the voice of God, how willingly we obey His Word, and how carefully we walk with those He has placed in our lives. Let us talk about what matters most.
