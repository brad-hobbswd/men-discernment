DISCERNMENT Men Who Know What Matters Week One | What Matters Most? Learning to Recognize What Deserves Our Attention Student Reading | Week One Introduction Life gives us plenty to think about. Work, family, responsibilities, relationships, decisions, church, and unexpected circumstances can fill our days before we have even had the opportunity to slow down. Most men understand responsibility. We know what needs to be done, what needs to be handled, and what people depend upon us to accomplish. Yet being busy and being spiritually focused are not always the same thing. Sometimes the things demanding our attention are not the things deserving the greatest portion of our attention. A disagreement can occupy our thoughts long after the conversation has ended. A frustration can affect our mood for the entire day. We may spend considerable time worrying about something beyond our control while neglecting the things God has placed directly in front of us. Discernment begins by learning to recognize the difference. Before we can discern situations, conversations, relationships, or decisions, we must begin understanding what matters most and whether our lives reflect those priorities. This week, we begin with a simple question: What is receiving my attention, and does that attention reflect what God desires to be important in my life? 1. Learning to Prioritize What Matters Scripture Reading Matthew 6:33 Jesus taught His disciples to seek first the Kingdom of God and His righteousness. The instruction was given while Jesus was addressing worry about everyday needs. Food, clothing, provision, and the uncertainty of tomorrow were real concerns. Jesus was not suggesting that those responsibilities were unimportant. He was teaching His disciples where their confidence and priorities needed to remain. God must never become something we fit into whatever time remains after everything else has received our attention. Our relationship with Jesus Christ is the foundation from which our lives should operate. Our responsibilities, relationships, work, family, and ministry must be understood within the context of our relationship with Him. When that foundation becomes neglected, other things can gradually begin occupying places they were never intended to hold. Prayer may become occasional. Bible reading may become inconsistent. Fellowship may begin feeling like an inconvenience. We may find ourselves so occupied with managing life that we give very little attention to our spiritual condition. The change often happens gradually. We may not make a conscious decision to move God out of first place. We simply begin allowing other things to take priority repeatedly. That is why spiritual priorities require intentional attention. Seeking God first does not mean neglecting the responsibilities He has given us. A man who loves God should also be faithful in his work, caring toward his family, responsible with his commitments, and dependable in his relationships. The question is whether those responsibilities are being carried with God at the center or whether they have gradually become the center themselves. 2. Our Attention Reveals What We Value Scripture Reading Proverbs 4:23 The Bible teaches us to guard our hearts because the issues of life flow from within. Our inner lives influence the way we think, speak, respond, and make decisions. What occupies our hearts often becomes visible through our daily behavior. Consider the things that repeatedly occupy your thoughts. Perhaps you are concerned about work. Perhaps a relationship has been difficult. Perhaps somebody’s words have hurt you. Perhaps you are trying to make a decision and cannot seem to stop thinking about the possible outcomes. Some matters genuinely deserve our attention. Others may be consuming more of our emotional energy than they deserve. Our attention is valuable. The things we continually focus upon can influence our attitudes, our peace, our relationships, and our spiritual lives. Guarding our hearts requires intentionality. We cannot allow every frustration, opinion, conflict, temptation, or distraction to determine the direction of our thoughts. This does not mean pretending that difficulties do not exist. It means learning to bring those difficulties before God and allowing His Word to help us process them. A man who guards his heart does not simply ignore what he feels. He learns to examine his feelings rather than allowing his feelings to govern his decisions. 3. Not Everything Deserves the Same Weight Scripture Reading Philippians 1:9–10 Paul prayed that the believers’ love would grow in knowledge and discernment so they could recognize and approve what was excellent. Discernment involves recognizing the difference between what is merely available for our attention and what is genuinely valuable. Not every disagreement deserves an extended argument. Not every criticism requires a response. Not every opportunity needs to be accepted. Not every problem needs to be solved immediately. Sometimes we give too much attention to matters that carry very little lasting significance. A man may spend an entire evening arguing about something that will have no meaningful effect upon his life while missing an opportunity to connect with his family. He may become consumed with proving his perspective while losing the opportunity to understand another person’s perspective. He may become so concerned about what others think that he loses sight of what God has called him to do. Discernment helps us evaluate the weight of a matter. Before responding, we can learn to ask ourselves: * Does this matter require my attention right now? * Is this something I can influence? * Is this conversation necessary? * Am I responding to the facts or to my emotions? * Will my response contribute to peace, understanding, accountability, or growth? * Am I allowing this matter to distract me from something more important? Wisdom is not demonstrated by having an opinion about everything. Sometimes wisdom is demonstrated by recognizing when something deserves our attention and when we need to release the matter and move forward. 4. Discernment Requires Practice Scripture Reading Hebrews 5:14 Scripture describes spiritual maturity as the result of exercising our ability to distinguish between good and evil. Discernment develops through practice. We grow as we apply the Word of God to real circumstances and allow the Lord to shape our understanding. Everyday situations provide opportunities for spiritual growth. When somebody frustrates us, we can practice patience. When somebody corrects us, we can practice humility. When we face uncertainty, we can practice seeking God. When we encounter temptation, we can practice choosing obedience. When a conversation becomes difficult, we can practice listening rather than reacting. When we make a mistake, we can practice taking responsibility rather than defending ourselves. Growth does not mean we will never struggle. Growth means we become increasingly willing to respond to the struggles of life according to the Word of God. A spiritually mature man is not a man who never makes mistakes. He is a man who remains teachable, recognizes when he needs correction, and continues allowing God to work within him. Discernment is strengthened through consistent obedience. 5. The Conversations That Matter Meaningful conversations do not always happen naturally. Sometimes we have to make room for them. Many men can discuss work, sports, current events, hobbies, responsibilities, and everyday circumstances. Those conversations have their place. Friendship is strengthened through laughter and shared experiences. However, there are also conversations that reach deeper. We may need to discuss our spiritual condition, our struggles, our fears, our decisions, our relationships, our responsibilities, and areas where we need help. Some men have become accustomed to answering, “I’m good,” even when they are struggling. There may be moments when we need somebody to listen without immediately trying to solve the problem. There may be moments when we need correction. There may be moments when we need encouragement or prayer. Healthy Christian brotherhood creates room for those conversations. Meaningful conversations require more than talking. They require listening, patience, compassion, honesty, and a willingness to understand. A man who listens carefully demonstrates respect. A man who asks thoughtful questions demonstrates interest. A man who speaks truth with compassion demonstrates maturity. A man who can receive correction without becoming defensive demonstrates humility. A man who recognizes when somebody needs encouragement demonstrates sensitivity. Discernment helps us recognize when an ordinary conversation may be an opportunity to strengthen somebody spiritually. We do not have to force every conversation to become deeply personal. We simply need to remain willing to recognize when something meaningful needs to be discussed. 6. When Our Priorities Need Adjustment Scripture Reading Romans 12:2 Our priorities can become misaligned without us immediately recognizing the change. Work may become our primary source of identity. Ministry may become something we use to measure our worth. Relationships may begin controlling our emotional well-being. Personal ambitions may become more important than obedience. Even good things can become unhealthy when they occupy the wrong place in our lives. A man may work hard to provide for his family while becoming emotionally absent from his home. A man may faithfully serve in ministry while neglecting his private prayer life. A man may spend considerable time discussing spiritual matters while becoming impatient with the people around him. These examples remind us that priorities require examination. The Lord may bring our attention to areas that need adjustment. Perhaps we need to spend more intentional time with our family. Perhaps we need to return to consistent prayer. Perhaps we need to stop allowing a particular frustration to dominate our thoughts. Perhaps we need to make room for a conversation that has been postponed. Recognizing the need for adjustment is not failure. Recognition gives us an opportunity to respond. God desires to transform our thinking so that our decisions and priorities reflect His will. We should remain willing to make adjustments whenever His Word reveals that something in our lives needs to change. 7. Becoming Men Who Know What Matters A man who develops discernment learns to approach life differently. He does not feel compelled to respond to every disagreement. He does not allow every frustration to control his mood. He recognizes that listening can be more valuable than speaking. He understands that asking for wisdom is a sign of strength. He is willing to acknowledge when he has misunderstood something. He values truth, yet understands the importance of speaking truth with compassion. He recognizes that spiritual growth requires humility and consistency. Most importantly, he learns to allow the Word of God to guide his priorities. Discernment is not about becoming critical of everyone around us. It is about becoming more spiritually aware, more responsible for our own actions, more sensitive to the leading of the Holy Ghost, and more committed to living according to Scripture. The purpose of this series is not simply to help us recognize what matters. The purpose is to help us live as men who demonstrate that we understand what matters. Our priorities, conversations, decisions, and relationships should increasingly reflect our commitment to Jesus Christ. The journey begins with awareness. Before we can make meaningful adjustments, we must first recognize where we are. Personal Reflection Take time to consider these questions privately. * What currently receives most of my time and attention? * What has occupied my thoughts the most recently? * Are my daily priorities reflecting my relationship with God? * What distractions have been affecting my spiritual focus? * Is there an important conversation I have been avoiding? * What is one area of my life that may need adjustment? * What would seeking God first look like in my everyday life this week? You are not required to share every personal answer with the group. The purpose is to honestly examine your own life before God. Homework Before Week Two An Examination of My Attention This week’s assignment is designed to help you become more aware of your priorities, thoughts, and reactions. The goal is not to judge yourself harshly, but to honestly recognize patterns that may reveal where growth is needed. Assignment One: Track Your Attention Choose three days before our next gathering. At the end of each day, spend five to ten minutes considering: * What consumed most of my time today? * What occupied most of my thoughts? * Did I intentionally make time for God? * Did I give meaningful attention to the people who matter in my life? * Was there something that distracted me unnecessarily? Write down a few observations. Assignment Two: Recognize a Reaction During the week, identify one moment when you felt frustrated, irritated, defensive, disappointed, or emotionally affected by something that happened. Write a brief account of the situation and consider: * What happened? * What was my immediate reaction? * Why did the situation affect me so strongly? * What did the moment reveal about what mattered to me? * How might I have responded with greater wisdom? You do not need to write a lengthy explanation. A few honest sentences are sufficient. Assignment Three: Have a Meaningful Conversation Before our next gathering, intentionally have one meaningful conversation with somebody in your life. Ask how they are genuinely doing. Give them time to answer. Listen without interrupting or immediately trying to provide a solution. Consider what you learned from the conversation and whether listening changed your understanding of the person. Assignment Four: Make One Adjustment Identify one practical change you can make this week to help bring your priorities into alignment. Examples include: * Establishing a more consistent prayer time. * Spending intentional time with family without distractions. * Reducing unnecessary time spent on social media. * Addressing a conversation you have been postponing. * Learning to pause before responding when frustrated. * Making time for Bible study and spiritual growth. Choose something realistic and put the adjustment into practice. Be Prepared for Week Two Come prepared to share one thing you discovered about yourself during this week’s homework. Next week, we will explore: WEEK TWO Discernment Begins Within Learning to Examine Our Own Hearts We will discuss how our motives, emotions, assumptions, fears, pride, and desires influence the way we interpret situations and respond to people. The first step toward discernment is recognizing what is happening within us. Before we learn to discern what is happening around us, we must become willing to allow God to reveal what is happening within us.
