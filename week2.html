SESSION TWO · STUDENT READING

Discernment Begins Within

Learning to Examine Our Own Hearts

Student Reading | Session Two

Psalm 139:23–24, James 1:19–20, Proverbs 16:2, Galatians 6:4

Introduction

Last session, we examined our priorities and considered where our attention was going. This session moves closer to home by examining what happens within us.

Our reactions, emotions, motives, expectations, and experiences influence the way we understand other people and situations. Sometimes our response has more to do with what we are carrying internally than with what actually happened.

Recognizing those influences requires honesty. Discernment is not only about identifying problems around us. Discernment also requires the willingness to recognize areas within ourselves that need attention.

Before examining someone else’s response, am I willing to examine my own?

1. Allow God to Examine Your Heart

Psalm 139:23–24

David invited God to examine his heart and reveal anything within him that needed correction. Such a prayer requires humility because we cannot grow while refusing to acknowledge areas needing improvement.

Self examination is not about searching for reasons to feel condemned. The purpose is to recognize what needs to change and allow God to lead us toward spiritual maturity.

We may discover impatience, pride, resentment, fear, insecurity, or motives that were not as pure as we initially believed.

Honest examination allows us to acknowledge those things rather than defend them.

2. Our Reactions Can Reveal Our Motives

Proverbs 16:2

Our actions may appear appropriate while our motives require examination.

We may offer advice because we genuinely want to help, or because we want control over someone else’s decision. We may become frustrated when corrected because the correction feels uncomfortable. We may serve faithfully while quietly expecting recognition.

The outward action does not always reveal the entire story.

Consider how you respond when someone disagrees with you, questions your decisions, or offers correction. Your reaction may reveal something worth examining.

Recognizing a motive that needs adjustment gives us an opportunity to respond with greater humility.

3. Feelings Deserve Attention, Not Control

James 1:19–20

Emotions are part of our human experience. Anger, disappointment, sadness, fear, and frustration can help us recognize that something matters to us. Wisdom requires learning how to respond rather than allowing emotions to determine our actions.

When we are frustrated, we may hear criticism differently. When we feel rejected, we may interpret someone’s silence as intentional. When we are embarrassed, we may become defensive before understanding what another person meant.

Taking time to pause can prevent an emotional response from becoming a harmful decision.

A wise response begins by asking what actually happened, what we are feeling, and whether our interpretation is accurate.

Personal Reflection

  • What reaction have I had recently that deserves closer examination?
  • Do I become defensive when someone corrects or disagrees with me?
  • Could my emotions or past experiences influence how I interpret others?
  • What motive or attitude might God be bringing to my attention?

Session Two Accountability

The Pause Before My Response

Today, identify one situation in which you experience a strong reaction.

Take a moment to examine:

  • What actually happened?
  • What did I feel?
  • What assumption did I make?
  • What did my reaction reveal about me?
  • How could I respond with greater wisdom?

Practice pausing before responding to a difficult situation. Your goal is to understand your own response before deciding what another person needs to change.

Come prepared to share:

What did I discover about myself when I took time to examine my reaction?

Preparing for Session Three

Learning to Listen Before Speaking

Understanding ourselves helps us recognize how our reactions influence conversations. Our next session will focus on listening carefully, understanding what another person is communicating, and learning when our words should wait.
