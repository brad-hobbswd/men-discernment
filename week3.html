SESSION THREE · STUDENT READING

Learning to Listen Before Speaking

The Discipline of Understanding

Student Reading | Session Three

Key Scriptures: James 1:19, Proverbs 18:13, Proverbs 20:5, Proverbs 15:1

Introduction

Many conversations become difficult not because someone lacks something to say, but because neither person has taken enough time to understand the other.

Listening requires more than hearing words. We must pay attention, resist the urge to interrupt, and remain willing to understand a perspective different from our own.

As men, we may feel pressure to solve problems, offer advice, defend ourselves, or provide an answer immediately. Sometimes the person speaking needs our wisdom. Other times, the greatest help we can offer is our attention.

Am I listening to understand, or listening to prepare my response?

Listen Before Responding

James 1:19

Scripture teaches the importance of being quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger.

That principle can change the way we handle conversations. When someone shares frustration, disappointment, or concern, our first response may be to explain, correct, defend, or offer a solution.

Responding too quickly can cause us to miss what the person is actually communicating.

Listening carefully allows us to understand the concern before deciding how to respond. A brief pause, a thoughtful question, or allowing someone to finish speaking can prevent unnecessary misunderstandings.

Listening does not mean agreeing with everything being said. It means respecting the conversation enough to understand before responding.

Understanding Requires Asking Questions

Proverbs 20:5

A person’s thoughts and concerns may not always be immediately apparent. Sometimes the most meaningful part of a conversation comes when we ask a question rather than make an assumption.

Instead of immediately saying, “You should have done this,” we might ask:

“Help me understand what happened.”

“What was most difficult about that situation?”

“What do you think you need right now?”

Questions create opportunities for understanding. They also demonstrate that we value the person speaking rather than simply waiting for an opportunity to offer our opinion.

A wise man recognizes that understanding often requires patience.

Our Words Can Help or Hurt

Proverbs 15:1

The way we respond matters. Truth spoken with unnecessary harshness can make a difficult conversation worse. A calm response can create space for honesty, even when disagreement exists.

Listening carefully also helps us recognize when a conversation requires encouragement, correction, compassion, or simply presence.

Not every conversation needs advice. Not every disagreement needs to be won. Sometimes a man serves another person best by remaining present, listening carefully, and responding with kindness.

Our words should reflect wisdom rather than impulsiveness.

Personal Reflection

  • Do I interrupt people or begin preparing my response before they finish?
  • How do I react when someone shares something I disagree with?
  • Do I tend to offer advice when someone simply needs to be heard?
  • What could change in my relationships if I became a better listener?

Session Three Accountability

Listen Without Interrupting

Today, intentionally have one conversation in which you practice listening.

Allow the other person to finish speaking without interruption. Ask at least one thoughtful question to help you understand. Resist the urge to immediately offer advice, defend yourself, or redirect the conversation toward your own experience.

Afterward, reflect:

What did I learn by listening that I might have missed had I responded too quickly?

Come prepared to share what you discovered about your listening habits.
