Why I Chose to Teach This I chose to develop this teaching because I believe there is tremendous value in creating spaces where men can talk about the things that matter beyond the surface of everyday life. We are often expected to carry responsibility, provide direction, make decisions, protect our families, serve our churches, fulfill our obligations, and remain dependable through difficult seasons. Those responsibilities are important, yet men also need opportunities to grow spiritually, ask questions, receive wisdom, and strengthen one another. There is a difference between having somebody nearby and having somebody who is willing to listen. There is a difference between being surrounded by people and having meaningful brotherhood. There is a difference between knowing Scripture and allowing Scripture to guide our responses when life becomes complicated. I believe discernment is an area of growth that affects nearly every part of our lives. The way we interpret a conversation can influence a relationship. The way we respond to correction can affect our character. The counsel we receive can influence our decisions. The motives we fail to examine can shape our actions without our realizing the impact. I wanted to create a teaching that would encourage men to slow down, consider Scripture, examine their hearts, and engage one another honestly. Rather than approaching every situation with an immediate conclusion, I want us to learn how to approach life with prayer, wisdom, humility, and a willingness to seek understanding. My desire is not to stand before men and suggest that I have mastered discernment. I am approaching this subject as somebody who recognizes the ongoing need to grow. We are all learning. We all encounter situations that challenge our understanding. We all need wisdom that comes from God. This teaching is an invitation to grow together. I believe God can use honest conversations, faithful friendships, biblical instruction, and prayer to strengthen men in ways that reach far beyond a weekly gathering. My hope is that the principles discussed during these eight weeks will continue working in our hearts long after the final lesson has been completed.
